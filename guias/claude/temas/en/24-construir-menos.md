I build more than I finish. I write it in the present tense on purpose.

With Claude Code, on top of that, the problem gets worse. When building a dashboard, a skill or an alert costs one afternoon, any idea becomes buildable, and a buildable idea looks like a good idea. The time that used to hold me back (because coding was slow) no longer holds anything back. The only thing left to stop me is me.

## The irony

I reread this guide end to end before closing it. There are cards on folders, templates, scheduled tasks, an automatic newsletter, a quality system, a spend meter, fictional advisors and mods. A lot of that is infrastructure. That is, things I built to work better that aren't the work. A guide on saving quota that took a considerable amount of quota to put together: the irony isn't lost on me, and I'd rather say it myself.

Part of that infrastructure paid for itself (the general file and the handoff, for sure). About another part I don't know yet. And some I built because it was fun, which is also a reason, even if it doesn't show up on any spreadsheet.

## The question

Now, before opening anything new, I ask myself one question:

> How much money or how many hours does this save me this week?

The important word is *this*. Almost anything saves something “someday”; the hard part is saving something in the next seven days. If the answer is “nothing, but it'll be really useful,” it's almost always a sign I'm building for the fun of it. Doing it for fun doesn't disqualify it, but then I file it in a different category and don't defend it as productivity.

The question has a trick: it forces you to measure. That's why I connect it with [Measuring spend](medir.html). Without a starting figure, any improvement is a feeling.

## What I'm trying now

- Before creating a project, a skill or an automation, I write in one line what it saves. If the line doesn't come, I don't create it.
- I close before I open. A half-done pending item costs more than one not started, because you have to load it into your head again.
- I prefer the smaller version. What repeats and has clear rules goes in a script, as I said in [Tasks](tareas.html) and in [Mods](mods.html), and what calls for judgment stays for the conversation. But there's also a version that comes before the script one, which is not doing it.
- I leave things unfinished on purpose, noted in the project's status, to pick up when there's quota and appetite, not when guilt is pushing me.

If you made it this far, you probably overbuild too. Pick one card, apply it, measure what changed and only then move on to another. The rest can wait on the shelf.

```text
Review what I have open across my projects (automations, skills, dashboards, scheduled tasks, mods) and put together a short table. For each thing: what it does, when I last used it and how much money or how many hours it saved me this week, according to what you can verify in the files. Mark whatever you couldn't verify as "no data" and don't make up figures. Then propose what to close, what to keep and what to archive, one at a time. Don't delete or change anything without my approval.
```
