Claude writes and designs with recognizable tics. None of them is wrong on its own. Together they make your site look like every site built with AI in the last year, and people already recognize them even if they can't name them.

## In the text

- “It's not X, it's Y” constructions.
- Headings that announce instead of saying: “What the sector was missing”.
- Neat closing lines that sound like a moral.
- Everything in groups of three.
- Dashes for dramatic pauses, and adjectives that say nothing.

Wikipedia keeps a long, well-documented list: [*Signs of AI writing*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing).

## In the design

- Tiny uppercase monospace labels above every block.
- The main heading inside a colored card with rounded corners.
- Rows of identical cards, all with the same shadow.
- A cream background with an elegant serif and a terracotta accent, or black with a single acid green.

That last one is mine: my site is black and white with an acid green. I back it up with print halftones and pixel creatures. Knowing something is a tic doesn't force you to drop it; it forces it to be a decision.

## Inventory, not rewrite

What works is asking Claude to **find** the tics, not fix them. Ask it to rewrite and it swaps one tic for another: it drops the “it's not X, it's Y” and puts in a rhetorical question. The rewriting is yours, in your voice. On my site I did it sentence by sentence in the short texts (buttons, subtitles, bios), which is where it showed most.

And an irony that isn't lost on me: I wrote this card with Claude.

```text
Make an inventory of AI-generated writing traits on the pages of this site. Don't touch texts signed by other people. Look for: “it's not X, it's Y”; triads; neat closing lines; headings that announce instead of saying; empty adjectives; dashes used as pauses; brochure tone. For each case: page, exact sentence and why it sounds like AI. Don't rewrite anything. Then, separately, do the same with the design: which visual choices are the ones an AI usually picks by default, and two alternatives for each that follow the site's identity.
```
